ProjectInformation processing ability of neural networks

As we age, or as neurons die, how quickly does the ability to learn change, and where does it collapse?

What I did and how

  • Formulated a mathematical foundation for a model’s information processing ability (IPA): how much loss it removes, divided by how fast it removes it.
  • Pruned networks step by step and measured IPA at each level, locating the elbow where learning collapses with the maximum of a smoothed second derivative.
  • Visualized and studied the phase transition of neurons with a visualization tool, and related it to phase transitions in physics.

Quantum Research

Quantum ML Research Assistant

Data Management Research Lab (DaMRL), FIU · with Prof. Janki BhimaniMay 2026 – Present

ProjectQuantum learning rates: Polyak step sizes with guarantees

A variational quantum algorithm walks down an energy landscape, and how far each step goes is usually hand-tuned. We derive it instead: the energy variance, measurable on the quantum computer, bounds the distance to the ground state, and a Polyak step turns that bound into the learning rate.

What I did and how

  • Two bounds set the step: Weinstein (E − λ₀ ≤ σ) for bold early steps, Temple (E − λ₀ ≤ σ²/Δ) for careful ones near the answer, switching where σ = Δ.
  • Implemented both branches of the step rule on SGD in PennyLane, with the Temple validity check E < λ₁ and a spectral gap Δ̂ re-estimated live from a regression of energy on variance.
  • Added a robust Theil–Sen estimator, a windowless moment estimator of Δ, and a min rule that needs no switch at all.
  • The schedule is equivalent to normalized imaginary-time evolution under Fubini–Study geometry.

By the numbers

0.05%from the exact ground energy; SGD stalled 7% above
0hand-tuned constants
14qubits in the largest benchmark
